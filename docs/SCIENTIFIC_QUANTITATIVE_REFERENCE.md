# Quantitative plugin reference

Each operation accepts finite numbers in the units shown in its form. Vector inputs represent ordered observations. Calculations use the supplied measurements directly. The examples below give reference values for checking a result by hand.

## Scientific Core

| Operation | Input contract and method | Reference result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Import numeric CSV (`numeric_csv`) | Supply a UTF-8 CSV text with distinct nonempty headings and up to 1,024 rows, 32 columns and 4,096 numeric cells. Quoted fields, blank lines, CRLF and a leading Unicode byte-order mark are supported. A cell that overflows, rounds a nonzero value to zero, or changes an integer count in binary64 produces a line-specific error. | A column containing `9007199254740993` produces an error on that row. The same column accepts the exactly representable count `9007199254740992`. |
| Convert a measurement (`convert_unit`) | Resolve case-sensitive SI symbols, exact prefixes and named derived units into seven SI base-dimension exponents; parse products, quotients and integer powers. Convert only compatible vectors. Absolute K/°C/°F use exact affine offsets (and enforce 0 K); explicit `delta_` units are temperature differences and cannot be mixed with temperature points. Original metric conversion outputs and saved receipts are unchanged. | 72 km/h = 20 m/s; 2 kWh = 7.2 MJ; 18 delta_degF = 10 delta_K; −273.15 °C = 0 K. `kg` to `m` and `K` to `delta_K` are errors. |
| Plot paired samples (`plot_xy`) | Supply finite, equal-length x and y vectors plus two nonempty axis labels with at most 96 characters each. The plot and table retain duplicate coordinates and preserve observation order. | x = `[2, 1, 2]` and y = `[5, 3, 7]` produce three connected points in that order. |

## Mathematics

| Operation | Input contract and method | Reference result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evaluate a polynomial (`polynomial`) | Supply 1–128 real coefficients in ascending power order and 1–256 sample coordinates. Horner’s method evaluates each point and switches to exact rational arithmetic near severe cancellation. The table preserves input order and the plot sorts by coordinate. | Coefficients `[2, -1, 3]` at `[2, -1, 0]` produce `[12, 6, 2]`. |
| Integrate sampled data (`trapezoid`) | Supply equally long x and y vectors, with at least two strictly increasing x values. Sum the signed trapezoid areas with exact binary rational intermediates before rounding the final integral. The result has units of x times units of y. | x = `[0, 1, 3]` and y = `[-2, 0, 4]` give an integral of 3. |
| Solve a linear system (`linear_system`) | Supply a square real matrix A of order 1–32 and a matching right-hand side b. Each equation is scaled by its largest coefficient before Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting. A pivot at or below n times machine epsilon in this scaled system causes a validation error. | `[[2, 1], [1, -1]]` with b = `[5, 1]` gives x = `[2, 1]` and a maximum absolute residual of zero. |

## Statistics

| Operation | Input contract and method | Reference result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Describe a sample (`describe`) | Supply at least two observations. The output includes count, mean, median, minimum, maximum and sample standard deviation with denominator n − 1. | `[1, 2, 3]` has mean 2, median 2 and sample standard deviation 1. |
| Fit a regression line (`linear_regression`) | Supply at least two paired observations with distinct predictor values. Ordinary least squares fits an intercept and slope. The plot spans the observed predictor range. For a constant response, the slope is zero and correlation and R² are undefined. | x = `[1, 2, 3]` and y = `[5, 3, 1]` give slope −2, intercept 7, Pearson r = −1 and R² = 1. |
| Summarize weighted observations (`weighted_summary`) | Supply paired observations and nonnegative relative weights, including at least one positive weight. The population variance uses the weighted sum of squared deviations divided by the total weight. Zero-weight observations contribute nothing. | Observations `[3, 5, 90]` with weights `[1, 1, 0]` give a weighted mean of 4 and population standard deviation of 1. |

## Physics

| Operation | Input contract and method | Reference result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model a spring oscillator (`spring_oscillator`) | Positive mass m (kg), positive stiffness k (N/m), and nonnegative displacement amplitude A (m). An undamped linear spring released from rest has period T = 2π√(m/k). The plot samples displacement A cos(2πt/T) at 65 points over one period. | m = 1 kg, k = 4 N/m, A = 0.1 m give T = π s and displacement −0.1 m at T/2. |
| Calculate heat flow through layers (`thermal_layers`) | Supply matching positive layer thicknesses (m) and conductivities (W/(m·K)), a common positive area (m²), and boundary temperatures (K). Sum each layer’s L/(kA) to obtain steady resistance. Positive heat flow travels from left to right. The model assumes perfect thermal contact. | For layers 0.1 m and 0.02 m thick, conductivities 0.2 and 0.04 W/(m·K), and area 2 m², total resistance is 0.5 K/W. Faces at 295 K and 275 K give 40 W. |
| Calculate sensible heat (`sensible_heat`) | With positive mass m (kg) and constant specific heat c (J/(kg·K)), heat transferred to a sample is mc(Tfinal − Tinitial). Temperatures are in kelvin. The model assumes a single phase throughout the interval. | A 2 kg sample with c = 400 J/(kg·K) cooling from 100 K to 90 K releases 8,000 J. The reported heat transfer is −8,000 J. |
| Solve damped and driven oscillator (`damped_driven_oscillator`) | Mass *m* (kg), stiffness *k* (N/m), damping *c* (N·s/m), drive force amplitude *F* (N), drive frequency (Hz), initial *x* (m), initial *v* (m/s), increasing nonnegative time (s) and resonance-scan frequency (Hz) vectors. Solve *mx″ + cx′ + kx = F cos(2πft)* with initial conditions via the complete homogeneous + sinusoidal particular solution, including the underdamped, critical and overdamped cases. A lossless exactly resonant drive uses the secular *t sin(ω₀t)* solution. The table reports time (s), displacement (m), velocity (m/s), acceleration (m/s²); the plot reports steady-state amplitude (m) versus frequency (Hz). The scalar damping ratio and Q are dimensionless (1); drive phase lag is in rad. Lossless resonance has unbounded steady-state amplitude: scans at the singular frequency are rejected. | For *m* = 1 kg, *k* = 100 N/m, *c* = 1 N·s/m, Q = 10 and the displacement resonance maximum is at √99.5/(2π) = 1.5875655713 Hz. Critical damping at *m* = 1 kg, *k* = 4 N/m, *c* = 4 N·s/m with *x(0)* = 1 m and *v(0)* = 0 yields *x(0.5 s)* = 2/e ≈ 0.7357588823 m. |
| Model projectile with quadratic air drag (`projectile_motion`) | Initial speed (m/s), elevation (deg, between −90 and 90), height (m), gravity (m/s²), mass (kg), air density (kg/m³), drag coefficient (1), cross-section (m²), maximum RK4 step (s), and maximum simulation duration (s). For drag: integrate *a = (0,−g) − (ρCdA/2m)\|v\|v* by RK4 with an adaptive bound on the drag step and bisection of ground impact and apex. With zero drag use the exact parabola. The result includes range and maximum height (m), flight time (s), impact speed (m/s), full sampled state table and trajectory. An impact outside the duration or the 100,000-step limit is an error rather than a truncated path. | Vacuum launch at 20 m/s and 45° from ground with conventional standard *g* = 9.80665 m/s² has range *v²/g* ≈ 40.78864852 m and time √2*v/g* ≈ 2.884192996 s. Vertical quadratic-drag ascent obeys *Δh* = ln(1 + bv₀²/g)/(2b), with *b = ρCdA/(2m)*. |
| Calculate rigid-body moment of inertia (`rigid_body_inertia`) | Select uniform `solid_sphere`, `thin_spherical_shell`, `solid_cylinder`, `solid_disk`, `thin_hoop`, `slender_rod`, or `rectangular_plate` (shape has unit 1). Enter mass (kg), radius (m), length (m), plate width (m), and nonnegative parallel-axis displacement (m); unused dimensions are ignored. The center axis is a diameter for a sphere/shell, symmetry axis for cylinder/disk/hoop, perpendicular bisector for rod and plate normal for the plate. Center/shifted inertia in kg·m², radius of gyration in m. | Uniform solid sphere *Icm* = 2mr²/5. At 3 kg, 2 m radius and 4 m parallel displacement, *Icm* = 4.8 kg·m² and *I* = 52.8 kg·m² by *I = Icm + md²*. A 12 kg, 2 m × 3 m plate has *Icm* = 13 kg·m². |
| Solve transient 1D heat conduction (`transient_heat_1d`) | Choose `explicit` or `implicit` (scheme, 1), positive slab length (m), thermal diffusivity (m²/s), uniform initial interior temperature and fixed left/right endpoint temperatures (K), grid nodes (integer, 3–256, unit 1), duration (s), and maximum time step (s). Centered spatial differences and forward Euler or backward Euler (Thomas tridiagonal solve) advance *∂T/∂t = α ∂²T/∂x²*. The Fourier number *Fo = αΔt/Δx²* is reported (1). Explicit updates reject Fo > 0.5; backward Euler is unconditionally stable. Up to 20,000 time steps; shortened final step reaches the requested duration. Profile and table are in m and K. | At three nodes with α = 0.1 m²/s, length 1 m, Δt = 0.5 s and initial interior 100 K, zero-K endpoints, Fo = 0.2: one explicit step gives 60 K, one implicit step gives 100/1.4 = 71.42857143 K. At finer grids, both converge to the standard odd-term Fourier sine series for a fixed-temperature slab. |
| Calculate Doppler shift (`doppler_shift`) | Select `sound` or `light` (1), emitted frequency (Hz), wave speed in medium (m/s; sound only), signed source speed toward receiver (m/s), signed receiver speed toward source (m/s). For sound, *f′/f = (c + vr)/(c − vs)* with both speeds subsonic; wavelength is in medium. For light, use relativistic collinear velocity addition and *f′/f = √[(1 + β)/(1 − β)]* with exact *c = 299792458 m/s*; reported wavelength is measured in the receiver frame. Outputs: observed frequency (Hz), wavelength (m), ratio and fractional shift (1). | Sound: 440 Hz, c = 343 m/s, source approaching at 20 m/s gives 467.2445820 Hz. Light: source approach at 0.6c and static receiver gives exactly 2× the emitted frequency. |
| Calculate relativistic energy and time (`relativistic_kinematics`) | Positive rest mass (kg), signed speed with \|v\| < 299792458 m/s, nonnegative clock proper interval (s). Outputs β and γ (1), dilated interval (s), signed momentum (kg·m/s), rest/kinetic/total energy (J). Compute *γ = 1/√(1 − β²)* and kinetic energy with a cancellation-safe expression at small speed. | At *v* = 0.6c, γ = 1.25 and 8 s of proper time corresponds to 10 s in the lab. With [2022 CODATA](https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?me) electron rest mass 9.1093837139 × 10⁻³¹ kg, *mc²* gives 0.51099895069 MeV to the published 11 decimal places (using exact 1 eV = 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ J). |
| Solve lenses, mirrors and Snell refraction (`geometric_optics`) | Choose `thin_lens`, `spherical_mirror`, or `snell` (1). Lens/mirror require signed nonzero focal length (m) and positive real-object distance (m): *1/f = 1/do + 1/di*, magnification *−di/do* (1). Positive focal length denotes converging lens or concave mirror; image distance (m) positive for real image. `snell` uses incident and transmitted indices (1) and incidence from normal (deg); reports refracted and reflected angles (deg), critical angle (deg, when n₁ > n₂) and TIR flag (1). An object exactly at the focus has image at infinity, represented as text with units. | *f* = 0.1 m, *do* = 0.3 m: *di* = 0.15 m and magnification −0.5. Refraction from *n₁* = 1.5 to *n₂* = 1 has critical angle arcsin(2/3) ≈ 41.81031490°. At 50° there is total internal reflection. |
| Model single/double slit intensity (`slit_diffraction`) | Choose `single` or `double` (1); enter wavelength (m), slit width (m), slit center separation (m, relevant for double), screen distance (m), central intensity (W/m²), and strictly increasing screen coordinates (m). Use exact screen geometry *sinθ = x/√(L² + x²)* with Fraunhofer *I/I₀ = sinc²(π a sinθ/λ)*, multiplied by *cos²(π d sinθ/λ)* for a coherent double slit. Table and plot include intensities in W/m²; relative intensity is unit 1. Paraxial fringe spacing (m) is an approximation. | For λ = 500 nm, *a* = 10 μm, *d* = 100 μm, *L* = 2 m, the first single-slit minimum occurs at arcsin(λ/a) ≈ 2.865983983° and the double-slit paraxial spacing is 0.0100 m. |
| Solve series RLC transient and AC response (`series_rlc_response`) | Series R (Ω), L (H), C (F), DC source step (V), initial capacitor voltage (V), initial current (A), AC voltage amplitude (V), nonnegative increasing time and frequency vectors (s, Hz). Solve *L C v″c + R C v′c + vc = Vstep* with full initial state, using all three damping regimes. Table gives capacitor voltage (V), series current (A), and stored electromagnetic energy (J) at each time (s). Plot gives sinusoidal steady-state current amplitude (A) versus frequency (Hz), with *Z = R + i(ωL − 1/ωC)*. Q (1) and half-power bandwidth (Hz) are reported; a lossless AC resonance is rejected as unbounded. | R = 10 Ω, L = 0.1 H, C = 0.001 F: natural frequency = 15.91549431 Hz, Q = 1 and half-power bandwidth = 15.91549431 Hz. A 1 V sinusoidal source produces 0.1 A at the natural frequency. |
| Analyze series RLC complex impedance (`series_rlc_frequency`) | Nonnegative series resistance R (Ω), positive inductance L (H) and capacitance C (F), sinusoidal voltage amplitude (V), and increasing nonnegative AC frequency samples (Hz). Phasor *Z = R + j(ωL − 1/ωC)* is evaluated at every frequency: frequency (Hz), impedance amplitude (Ω), impedance phase (rad) and current amplitude (A) in the table; the plot shows current amplitude (A) vs frequency (Hz). A positive impedance phase denotes inductive reactance, negative denotes capacitive; current phase is opposite to impedance phase. The DC capacitor acts as an open circuit (infinite impedance). Exact lossless resonance with a nonzero voltage is rejected as unbounded. | For R = 10 Ω, L = 0.1 H, C = 0.001 F, the resonance frequency is 15.91549431 Hz, where the impedance is 10 Ω at phase 0 rad and the 1 V source current amplitude is 0.1 A. The impedance phase is negative below and positive above resonance. |


The extended models are deterministic, standard-library-only idealizations, not CFD/FEM or measured-material databases. Sample vectors are bounded by the Workbench's normal input and saved-result limits. Strings such as “infinite (m)” and “not applicable (deg)” mark singular or physically absent outputs; they are not silently returned as finite values. The tests compare these models with closed-form harmonic, ballistic, drag-ascent, slab Fourier-series, geometrical optics and RLC benchmarks, plus the published constants below. For the 2022 CODATA electron rest-energy validation, the test uses a tolerance of ±5 × 10⁻¹² MeV (the last quoted decimal place) and ±5 × 10⁻¹² relative error in joules; actual uncertainties in measured constants remain those given by CODATA.

Sources: [NIST SI exact light speed and elementary charge](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-330/sp-330-section-2) · [NIST conventional standard free-fall acceleration, 9.80665 m/s²](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-appendix-b-conversion-factors/nist-guide-si-appendix-b9) · [NIST 2022 CODATA electron mass and rest energy](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/pdf/JPCRD2022CODATA.pdf).


## Astronomy

| Operation | Input contract and method | Reference result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Estimate distance from parallax (`parallax_distance`) | A precise, positive annual parallax p in milliarcseconds gives distance 1,000/p parsecs. The operation also reports light-years. For noisy measurements, inverse parallax alone can be a poor distance estimate. | A parallax of 100 mas gives 10 pc, or approximately 32.6156 ly. |
| Calculate a photometric distance (`distance_modulus`) | Supply apparent magnitude m, absolute magnitude M and nonnegative extinction A in a common passband. The true modulus μ = m − M − A gives distance 10^(1 + μ/5) parsecs. | m = 8 mag, M = 1 mag and A = 2 mag give μ = 5 mag and distance 100 pc. |
| Find a blackbody spectral peak (`blackbody_peak`) | A positive temperature T (K) gives the peak of spectral radiance per unit wavelength by λmax = b/T. The operation uses b = 2.897771955 × 10⁻³ m·K and reports nanometres. | A 3,000 K blackbody peaks at approximately 965.924 nm. |

The parallax convention and Wien displacement constant are documented by ESA and NIST. [ESA stellar parallax](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Gaia/Parallax) · [NIST Wien wavelength displacement constant](https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?bwien)

## Engineering

| Operation | Input contract and method | Reference result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Combine ideal resistors (`resistor_network`) | Supply at least one nonnegative resistance in ohms. The operation reports the sum for a series circuit and the reciprocal sum of conductances for a parallel circuit. A zero-ohm branch shorts a parallel circuit. | 10 Ω and 30 Ω give 40 Ω in series and 7.5 Ω in parallel. |
| Plot an RC step response (`rc_step`) | Positive resistance R (Ω), positive capacitance C (F), voltage step V (V), and increasing sample times t ≥ 0 (s). An initially uncharged capacitor has voltage V(1 − exp(−t/RC)). The table and plot use the supplied times. | R = 1,000 Ω, C = 0.001 F and V = 5 V give a time constant of 1 s and a capacitor voltage of approximately 3.16060 V at 1 s. |
| Calculate thermal expansion (`linear_expansion`) | Positive initial length L (m), constant linear expansion coefficient α (1/K), and uniform signed temperature change ΔT (K). Unconstrained small-strain expansion gives ΔL = LαΔT. The final length must remain positive. | A 2 m bar with α = 0.000023/K warming by 40 K extends 0.00184 m to 2.00184 m. |
