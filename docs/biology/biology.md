# Scientific Workbench — Biology and Ecology reference

The Biology pack retains the **three original operation IDs, fields, examples and exact outputs** (`community_diversity`, `quadrat_density`, `community_overlap`). Existing `science_biology.workbench` nodes, source bindings, saved receipts, and shared pack-version behavior have not changed. Eleven additional operations are appended (including mark–recapture, closed-cohort life tables and structured-population projections); none requires NumPy, SciPy, R, a web service, or any package beyond the Python standard library and Graphlink's existing scientific fitting core.

## Operation methods, citations, and categorical inputs

Every Biology and Ecology catalog entry now supplies a **Methods and citations** section in the Scientific Workbench card. The method describes the **implemented** estimator or numerical scheme and its limitations, with links to primary publications or an authoritative equation/reference description. These citations refer to the analytical methods, not to the origin of an individual user's data and are not an external validation or a guarantee that model assumptions hold.

The `dose_response_4pl_fit` operation now declares `potency_metric` as a **choice** field, with exactly `EC50` and `IC50` as allowed values; the editor shows them in a dropdown and the calculation backend rejects other values. The example remains `EC50`. This selection is a **label for the relative half-maximal concentration** of the fitted four-parameter logistic curve, not a switch to a different fitting algorithm. Existing operation identifiers, other input keys, calculations, output fields and saved-result receipt formats are unchanged.

## Input, output, and statistical conventions

Every new input is labeled with its physical unit, or explicitly says it is categorical/dimensionless. For instrument-specific units, enter a **unit label** alongside the data, e.g. `days`, `CFU/mL`, `µM`, `nmol/min/mg`, `% inhibition`. The program **does not convert between user-supplied unit labels**. Quantitative results, table headers, and graph axes use those units; scalar-value dictionaries carry companion `*_unit` fields. Rates use reciprocal time units, residual sums of squares use squared response units, and probabilities/Hill slopes/R²/chi-square values are dimensionless. Never mix different units in one input vector. The three original operations deliberately preserve their historical schemas and representations rather than retroactively rewriting saved results.

All fitted models minimize **unweighted** residual sum of squares in **response units squared** (each replicate is a separate observation). They reuse Graphlink's high-precision Decimal Levenberg–Marquardt solver in `graphlink_plugins.scientific.packs.fitting`; positivity constraints and analytic derivatives are handled by that solver. A successful fit requires more points than free parameters and an identifiable, sufficiently broad experimental design. Standard errors are the square roots of diagonal terms of the local **SSE/(n − p) × (JᵀJ)⁻¹** covariance. They are linearized, conditional estimates, **not** biological-replicate variance estimates or bootstrap confidence bounds; their reliability decreases for strong parameter correlation and weakly sampled asymptotes. The workbench includes residual tables and fitted-series plots. Unchanged provenance checksums and input fingerprints apply to all operations.

**Growth doubling time** is calculated from the modeled abundance **at the first observed time** to twice that abundance, when `2N(first) < K`. For logistic growth the elapsed time is `ln(2(K−N0)/(K−2N0))/r`; for Gompertz it is `ln(ln(K/N0)/ln(K/(2N0)))/r`. If that level is beyond the carrying capacity, the result is labeled *not reached*, rather than reporting an incorrect `ln(2)/r` shortcut. The two growth models fit sorted observed pairs and retain observed/fitted/residual values at every time.

**4PL at zero dose:** `ln(0)` is undefined. Zero-dose controls are retained in the residual objective by using a log concentration 500 natural-log units below the lowest positive dose. The bound `0.1 ≤ h ≤ 20` makes the resulting fitted value indistinguishable from the analytical low-dose limit at normal floating-point precision. Concentration values must be nonnegative and include at least four *distinct strictly positive* doses. For data with slopes flatter than 0.1 or more than 20, use a different model / experimental design. The potency CI is the exponentiated linearized Student-t interval for `ln(c50)`; the `c50` standard error is delta-method `c50 × SE(ln(c50))`.

**Survival risk sets:** each individual contributes to risk at its observed time, whether event or right-censoring. At any joint event time, group-A's expected events are `d nA/n` and variance is `nA nB d(n−d)/(n²(n−1))` when `n>1`. The log-rank statistic is `(Σ(OA−EA))² / Σvariance`, with **df=1** and right-tail probability. The test is not the naive sum of `(O−E)²/E` contributions, which gives a different answer for censored samples. Medians are the first time KM survival reaches or falls below 0.5; otherwise *not reached*. Greenwood standard errors refer to the final reported KM survival probabilities; there is no stratification, competing-risk treatment, or proportional-hazards assumption built into the statistic.

**Rarefaction numerical evaluation:** for each observed taxon of abundance `nᵢ` in a total `N`, `E[Sₘ] = Σ(1 − C(N−nᵢ,m)/C(N,m))`. Calculations use stable log products and `expm1`; for very long products with a small occupancy, a curvature-corrected midpoint log-product approximation avoids catastrophic cancellation in differences of large log-gamma values. Presence terms with absence probability below exp(−40) are rounded to 1. The returned table/plot samples at most 27 subsample sizes (always including `1`, the requested `m`, and `N`) and is an **expected-value** curve, not simulated or a confidence interval. These are binary64 calculations; for extreme surveys requiring more than binary64 precision, use arbitrary-precision analysis.

## Population dynamics additions

The following three operations extend the existing Biology and Ecology grant (science_biology), not a separate plugin. Every result includes the ordinary Workbench summary, typed scalar values, input-unit-aware table, numeric plot and provenance receipt. They require no SciPy, NumPy or online service.

### Two-capture mark–recapture

Enter M, the number marked and released in the first catch; C, the total second catch; and R, the number of already-marked individuals in that catch. Both catches must be positive, and R must be between zero and min(M,C). The uncorrected Lincoln–Petersen estimate MC/R is undefined when R=0. The default, more robust Chapman population estimate is (M+1)(C+1)/(R+1)−1 with estimated variance (M+1)(C+1)(M−R)(C−R)/[(R+1)²(R+2)]. Standard error is the square root of this variance. When R≥5, an illustrative normal-approximation 95% interval is Chapman ± 1.96 SE, with its lower bound clipped at M+C−R, the distinct animals actually encountered. This is **not an exact confidence interval**, and its small-sample coverage may be poor. With R<5, the interval is explicitly undefined, while the Chapman point estimate is shown with a warning.

This method requires population closure between catches, thorough mixing, equal catchability, tags that do not affect behavior or survival, and no tag loss. Migration, heterogeneous detectability and short recapture windows can bias the results. The OpenStax example M=80, C=100, R=20 produces Lincoln–Petersen 400 individuals; Chapman is 81×101/21−1 ≈ 388.571 individuals. See [OpenStax Population Demography](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/45-1-population-demography) and the [Chapman estimator](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/recapr/html/NChapman.html) / [variance](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/recapr/html/vChapman.html) references.

### Closed-cohort life table

Enter strictly increasing age boundaries beginning at zero, cohort survivors nₓ at **every** boundary, and age-interval fecundity mₓ in offspring per survivor. The cohort must be entirely deceased at the final boundary, with terminal mₓ=0; a right-truncated cohort must not be treated as complete. The calculation returns interval deaths dₓ=nₓ−nₓ₊₁, survivorship lₓ=nₓ/n₀, mortality probability qₓ=dₓ/nₓ, person-time Lₓ=(nₓ+nₓ₊₁)Δage/2, future person-time Tₓ=Σₖ≥ₓ Lₖ, and expected remaining life eₓ=Tₓ/nₓ. Where no survivors remain, conditional probabilities and life expectancy are *undefined*, not zero. Trapezoidal person-time assumes evenly distributed deaths within each interval; unequal interval widths are supported.

The reproductive summary computes R₀=Σ lₓmₓ, generation time G=(Σ ageₓlₓmₓ)/R₀ when R₀>0, and the *approximation* ln(R₀)/G when G>0. This last statistic is not a fitted Euler–Lotka intrinsic growth parameter and should not be treated as such. For ages [0,1,2,3], survivors [100,80,50,0] and fecundity [0,2,1,0], Lₓ=[90,65,25,0] individual-years, life expectancy at age zero is 1.8 years and R₀=2.1 offspring per original individual. See the [OpenStax life-table reference](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/45-1-population-demography).

### Age- or stage-structured matrix projection

Supply a nonnegative square projection matrix A, the matching initial-stage abundances and a finite number of steps. Each stage is a row of the matrix and an element of the abundance vector, with columns specifying contributing source stages. The Workbench iterates nₜ₊₁=A nₜ; the first row of the table is the supplied state at t=0, followed by one row per time step. Fertility and survival/transition coefficients are nonnegative per-step values, and stage abundances are **expected** real counts, not rounded individuals. Both Leslie age-class and Lefkovitch stage-class matrices are supported. Total abundance and each stage are tabulated; total abundance is plotted.

The finite-horizon geometric multiplier (Nₜ/N₀)^(1/t) is distinct from the dominant eigenvalue. A separate normalized power iteration attempts to determine the Perron multiplier λ and stable stage distribution, but publishes them only when an eigenpair is verified to have converged. Periodic or slowly converging matrices still produce exact finite-step trajectories, with asymptotic results explicitly labeled undefined. Assumptions are time-invariant vital rates, no density feedback and no demographic stochasticity. For Leslie A=[[1,1],[1,0]] and n₀=[1,0], total abundance at t=0…5 is [1,2,3,5,8,13], with dominant multiplier (1+√5)/2≈1.618034. See [Leslie (1945), Biometrika 33:183–212](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/33.3.183).

Independent computational checks for all three operations, including zero overlaps, censored/invalid cohorts, irregular age intervals, matrix extinction and periodic matrices, live in backend/tests/test_scientific_population_dynamics.py.

**Scope and interpretation:** these procedures are suitable for exploratory experimental/field analysis and education, not automatic approval of model assumptions. Do not interpret very small p-values as effect size, fit an unidentifiable plateau, infer a *constant* doubling time from density-limited growth, or interpret Chao1 as a census of all taxa. For KM or genotype data with sparse counts, consider exact or domain-specific analyses elsewhere. Neither new operation IDs nor their output structures alter older saved workbench results.

### Independent numerical correctness audit: extreme-size rarefaction

Individual-based rarefaction still uses **Hurlbert's without-replacement expectation** `E[S_m]=Σ_i[1−C(N−n_i,m)/C(N,m)]` (taxa); this is not extrapolation. For enormous survey totals, subtracting four `lgamma` terms loses the small per-taxon detection probability even when that probability is representable. Evaluation now uses a `log1p` product and `expm1` for short products, a curvature-corrected midpoint estimate for extremely long products when the expected detection occupancy is small, and an analytically negligible absence bound when the occupancy exceeds 40. For instance `N=2**53−1`, a taxon count of 3,000, and sample size 3,000 retain its approximately `9.9920072×10⁻¹⁰` contribution to expected taxa; the prior `lgamma` subtraction gave zero. Count constraints and reported units remain unchanged. Reference: Hurlbert (1971), *Ecology* 52(4), 577–586; [individual-based rarefaction formulation](https://cran.csiro.au/web/packages/spacc/vignettes/rarefaction-standardization.html).
